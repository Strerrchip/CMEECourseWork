Initial conditions strongly affect early richness: the maximally diverse community loses species, while the single-species community gains species through speciation. With the same community size and speciation probability, the initial-condition effect weakens over time as both communities approach the same stationary distribution of richness. They do not have to follow identical paths or reach the same richness at a particular generation. Unlike the model without speciation, monodominance is not absorbing because new species can appear.
